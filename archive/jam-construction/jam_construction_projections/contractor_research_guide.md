# Research Guide: Twin Cities Contractor Database Development

## Ethical Research Methods for Contractor Contact Information

### 1. Public Databases and Directories

#### Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry (DLI)
- Contractor license database: https://mn.gov/dli/licensing/
- Search for licensed contractors in Twin Cities area
- Filter by license type and business size
- Export available data (company name, address, license status)
- Contact information often available through follow-up research

#### Minnesota Secretary of State Business Directory
- Business registration information
- Officer/owner names and addresses
- Company formation details
- Contact information when available

#### City Business License Databases
- Minneapolis: https://lims.minneapolismn.gov/
- St. Paul: https://www.stpaul.gov/
- Search for construction business licenses
- Often includes company contact information

#### City of Minneapolis Subcontractor Registry
- Small and underutilized businesses database
- Includes Disadvantaged Business Enterprise (DBE)-certified companies
- Covers construction and other services
- Geographic scope: Anoka, Carver, Chisago, Dakota, Hennepin, Isanti, Le Sueur, Mille Lacs, Ramsey, Scott, Sherburne, Sibley, Washington, and Wright counties in Minnesota; Pierce and St. Croix counties in Wisconsin
- Contact: Johnnie Burns, Associate Director, Contract Compliance Division
- Address: City Hall, 350 Fifth St. S., Room 239, Minneapolis, MN 55415
- Office hours: 8:30 a.m. – 4:30 p.m., Monday – Friday
- Search for DBE-certified construction contractors in the Twin Cities area

### 2. Industry Association Directories

#### Associated General Contractors (AGC) of Minnesota
- Member directory with company profiles
- Company size and specialty information
- Contact persons and company details
- Membership indicates serious industry commitment

#### Minnesota Construction Association (MCA)
- Contractor member directory
- Government contracting focus (relevant for your expansion)
- Company profiles with specialties

#### Home Builders Association of Minnesota
- Residential contractor listings
- Builder and remodeler directories
- Contact information for residential market

### 3. Online Business Directories

#### Google My Business
- Search "construction contractors Minneapolis"
- Filter by ratings and company size indicators
- Often includes contact information directly
- Customer reviews provide market intelligence

#### Yellow Pages and Superpages
- Construction category searches
- Filter by Twin Cities metro area
- Often includes phone numbers and addresses

#### Angie's List / Handy
- Contractor profiles with business information
- Customer rating information
- Service area confirmation

### 4. Government Project Research

#### Minnesota Department of Transportation (MnDOT)
- Current projects database
- Contractor information for active projects
- Bid results showing successful contractors
- Subcontractor information often available

#### GovernmentBid.com and FedRooms
- Government project announcements
- Contractor prequalification lists
- Bid results showing winning contractors
- Upcoming opportunities to target

### 5. Trade Publication Directories

#### Midwest Construction Magazine
- Contractor ranking lists
- Project feature articles with contractor names
- Awards and recognition lists
- Market intelligence for larger contractors

#### Minneapolis/St. Paul Business Journal
- Contractor market rankings
- Top contractor lists by revenue
- Project announcements with contractor names

### 6. Research Framework for 200+ Contractors

#### Target Profile Definition:
- Company size: $2M-$50M annual revenue (estimated)
- Employee count: 10-50 employees
- Geographic focus: Twin Cities metro area
- Services: General construction, specialty trades
- Years in business: 3+ years (established)

#### Classification System:
- **Tier 1**: Large contractors ($20M+ revenue) - 50 companies
- **Tier 2**: Mid-size contractors ($5M-$20M) - 100 companies
- **Tier 3**: Small established contractors ($2M-$5M) - 75 companies
- **Tier 4**: Emerging contractors ($1M-$2M) - 50 companies

#### Data Points to Collect:
- Company name
- Primary contact name and title
- Phone number
- Email address (often info@[company], contact@[company])
- Website
- Annual revenue (estimated)
- Employee count (estimated)
- Primary services
- Geographic service area
- Years in business
- License numbers
- Insurance carrier (if publicly available)

### 7. Contact Verification Process

#### Primary Research Methods:
- Visit company website directly
- Call company main line and ask for estimating contact
- Check LinkedIn for key personnel
- Search for company in news articles
- Verify business address and status

#### Quality Assurance:
- Verify business is active and in good standing
- Confirm company provides services you can support
- Ensure geographic area aligns with your service area
- Validate contact person is appropriate for estimating services

### 8. Outreach Strategy Once Compiled

#### Initial Contact Methods:
- Direct phone call to introduce services
- Professional email introduction
- LinkedIn connection requests
- Direct mail (for local companies)

#### Value Proposition Alignment:
- For mid-size contractors: "Free up your estimator's time for bigger projects"
- For smaller contractors: "Access to commercial estimating expertise without hiring costs"
- For government contractors: "Prevailing wage and compliance expertise"

### 9. Tools to Assist Research

#### Spreadsheet Software:
- Track all collected information
- Sort and filter by criteria
- Track contact attempts and responses
- Schedule follow-up activities

#### CRM Options:
- HubSpot Free (good for list management)
- Pipedrive (simple contact management)
- Insightly (contact and project tracking)

#### Contact Verification Tools:
- Hunter.io (find and verify email addresses)
- Voila Norbert (find contact information)
- Clearbit Connect (enrich contact information)

### 10. Legal and Ethical Guidelines

#### CAN-SPAM Compliance:
- Only contact businesses with legitimate interest
- Provide clear opt-out mechanisms
- Use accurate sender information
- Honor opt-out requests immediately

#### Privacy Considerations:
- Only use publicly available information
- Don't purchase lists from unknown sources
- Respect company preferences regarding contact
- Follow industry association guidelines

#### Professional Ethics:
- Don't misrepresent services or company
- Be transparent about business purpose
- Respect Do Not Call registries
- Follow up professionally without harassment

### 11. Research Schedule Recommendation

#### Week 1-2: Database Mining
- Focus on public databases (DLI, Secretary of State)
- Industry association directories
- Government project databases

#### Week 3-4: Online Research
- Business directories and Google My Business
- Trade publication research
- LinkedIn research for contact persons

#### Week 5-6: Verification and Validation
- Call companies to verify information
- Confirm appropriate contact persons
- Begin initial outreach to top prospects

This systematic approach will help you build a quality database of Twin Cities contractors through legitimate means, providing a strong foundation for your business development efforts.
