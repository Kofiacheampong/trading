# Goat Industry Research — Ghana & West Africa

## Key Data Points Collected (June 16, 2026)

### Market Size
- Ghana goat population: ~8 million head (USDA 2023)
- Ghana mutton/goat meat market volume: 47,000 metric tonnes (2023)
- Ghana imported $5.44M of sheep/goat meat in 2024 — demand exceeds domestic supply
- Goat meat market demand in Ghana: ~$2.5M/year in formal trade (more informal)
- Global goat meat market: $248B (2026) → $317B (2035), CAGR 2.8%
- Global goat farming market: $8.7B (2025) → $13.2B (2033), CAGR 5.3%

### Pricing (Ghana 2026)
- Live local goat (adult): GHS 800-1,500
- Live Boer goat: GHS 1,500-3,000+
- Boer breeding doe: GHS 2,000-3,500
- Boer buck: GHS 3,000-5,000
- Goat meat retail: GHS 40-90/kg
- Live goat price per kg: roughly GHS 25-50/kg live weight
- Imported Boer/Kalahari can reach $1,500-2,000 per animal

### Production Context
- Most goats in Ghana raised in traditional extensive systems (low input, low output)
- Small herd sizes (5-20 per household)
- High mortality from disease, poor nutrition, lack of vet care
- Formal commercial goat farming is rare
- Livestock contributes ~12% of Ghana's GDP

### Boer Goat Advantage
- Local breeds: 25-35 kg adult, 12-18 months to market
- Boer: 90-120 kg adult, 8-10 months to market
- Better kidding rates, higher meat yield, premium prices

### Value Chain Issues (relevant to any West African context)
- Informal/fragmented marketing
- Poor access to extension services
- Weak farmer bargaining power
- Dependence on middlemen
- Seasonal price fluctuations
- Strong farmer demand for training (86-88% want capacity building)
