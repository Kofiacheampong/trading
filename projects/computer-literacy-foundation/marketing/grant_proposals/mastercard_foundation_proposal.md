- 3 sustainable learning centers established

## PROBLEM STATEMENT

### The Digital Divide in Rural Ghana

Ghana has made significant progress in digital infrastructure, but rural communities remain underserved:

**Statistics:**
- Only 22% of rural Ghanaians have basic digital literacy skills (vs. 58% urban)
- Rural youth unemployment: 28% (vs. 18% urban)
- 67% of jobs now require basic digital skills
- Only 15% of rural schools have functional computer labs
- Internet penetration: 35% rural vs. 68% urban

**Barriers Faced by Rural Youth:**
1. **Access:** No nearby training facilities; must travel to urban centers (costly)
2. **Affordability:** Training costs prohibitive for low-income families
3. **Relevance:** Existing curricula not aligned with local job markets
4. **Infrastructure:** Unreliable electricity and internet in rural areas
5. **Gender Gap:** Cultural barriers limit girls' and women's participation in tech

**Consequences:**
- Limited employment opportunities
- Continued rural-urban migration
- Perpetuation of poverty cycles
- Underutilized human capital
- Widening inequality

### Community Need Assessment

[Foundation Name] conducted preliminary assessments in [Target Communities/Region]:

**Key Findings:**
- 85% of youth expressed interest in computer training
- 72% believe digital skills would improve job prospects
- 90% willing to travel up to 5km for training
- Top desired skills: Microsoft Office, internet usage, digital marketing, basic coding
- Employers cite lack of digital skills as major hiring barrier

**Testimonials:**
> "I want to work in an office, but every job requires computer skills. There's nowhere to learn here." - *Kwame, 23, [Community]*

> "If I had computer skills, I could grow my small business. I can't afford to go to the city for training." - *Ama, 28, Market Trader*

## PROJECT DESCRIPTION

### Goal

To enable 1,500 rural youth in Ghana to access dignified and fulfilling work opportunities through comprehensive digital skills training and employment support.

### Objectives

1. **Establish Infrastructure:** Set up 3 fully-equipped computer learning centers in rural communities by Month 6

2. **Deliver Training:** Train 1,500 youth (ages 15-29) in digital literacy and job-relevant skills over 18 months

3. **Facilitate Employment:** Achieve 55% job placement or self-employment rate within 6 months of graduation

4. **Promote Inclusion:** Ensure 70% female participation and 10% persons with disabilities

5. **Build Sustainability:** Develop revenue-generating activities to ensure center sustainability beyond grant period

### Target Beneficiaries

**Primary:** Youth ages 15-29 in rural communities
- 70% women and girls
- 10% persons with disabilities
- 30% from extremely low-income households (full scholarships)

**Secondary:**
- Local employers (access to skilled workforce)
- Communities (economic development)
- Families (increased household income)

**Geographic Focus:** [Specify Region/Districts - prioritize areas near your operations]

### Program Components

#### Component 1: Learning Center Setup (Months 1-6)

**Activities:**
- Lease and renovate 3 community spaces (100-150 m² each)
- Install 15-20 computers per center (45-60 total)
- Set up reliable internet connectivity (fiber/Starlink)
- Install backup power systems (solar/generator)
- Furnish classrooms and offices
- Recruit and train center managers and trainers

**Outputs:**
- 3 operational learning centers
- 60 computers installed and functional
- 15 staff trained and deployed

#### Component 2: Digital Skills Training (Months 6-24)

**Curriculum Tracks:**

**Track A: Basic Digital Literacy (6 weeks)**
- Computer fundamentals
- Operating systems
- File management
- Internet and email
- Digital safety
- *Target: 600 students*

**Track B: Productivity & Office Skills (8 weeks)**
- Microsoft Word/Google Docs
- Microsoft Excel/Google Sheets
- Microsoft PowerPoint/Google Slides
- Document formatting
- Basic data analysis
- *Target: 500 students*

**Track C: Digital Skills for Employment (10 weeks)**
- Advanced Office applications
- Online job searching
- Resume/CV creation
- Email professionalism
- Video conferencing
- Cloud tools
- Social media for business
- *Target: 300 students*

**Track D: Specialized Skills (12 weeks)**
- Digital marketing
- Basic web development
- Graphic design (Canva)
- E-commerce basics
- Entrepreneurship skills
- *Target: 100 students*

**Training Approach:**
- Hands-on, practical learning (70% practical, 30% theory)
- Flexible scheduling (morning, afternoon, evening, weekend)
- 1 computer per 2 students maximum
- Certified trainers with local knowledge
- Continuous assessment and feedback
- Certificates upon completion

#### Component 3: Employment Support (Ongoing)

**Activities:**
- Career counseling and guidance
- Resume and interview preparation
- Job matching with employer partners
- Internship placements
- Entrepreneurship support for self-employment
- Alumni network development
- 6-month post-graduation follow-up

**Employer Partnerships:**
- MOUs with 30+ local employers
- Job fairs and recruitment events
- Skills advisory input from employers
- Internship and apprenticeship programs

#### Component 4: Community Engagement (Ongoing)

**Activities:**
- Community awareness campaigns
- Parent and leader engagement
- Success story documentation
- Community advisory committees
- Free community digital literacy days
- School outreach programs

### Implementation Timeline

| Phase | Months | Key Activities |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **Setup** | 1-6 | Legal registration, site selection, center setup, staff hiring, curriculum finalization |
| **Launch** | 6-9 | First cohort enrollment, training begins, employer partnerships established |
