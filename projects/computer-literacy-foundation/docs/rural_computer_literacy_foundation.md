## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This document outlines the plan to establish a non-profit foundation focused on bringing computer literacy and digital skills training to rural areas of Ghana. The foundation will address the digital divide by providing accessible, affordable, and practical technology education to underserved communities.

**Key Objectives:**
- Train 500-1,000 students in Year 1
- Establish 3-5 learning centers in rural communities
- Achieve 70%+ course completion rate
- Place 50%+ of graduates in jobs or further education
- Build sustainable funding model (grants, partnerships, donations)

**Initial Investment:** GHS 150,000-300,000  
**Annual Operating Budget:** GHS 200,000-400,000  
**Funding Sources:** Grants, corporate partnerships, individual donors, government support

## 2. LEGAL STRUCTURE & REGISTRATION

### 2.1 Foundation Structure Options

**Option A: Non-Profit Organization (NGO)**
- Registered with Department of Social Welfare
- Tax-exempt status possible
- Can receive donations and grants
- More regulatory compliance required

**Option B: Company Limited by Guarantee**
- Registered with Registrar General's Department
- Non-profit structure
- Easier to operate than NGO
- Good for corporate partnerships

**Option C: Trust**
- Established under trust law
- Trustees manage foundation
- Flexible structure
- Common for charitable foundations

**Recommended:** **Option B (Company Limited by Guarantee)** - Easier to establish and operate while maintaining non-profit status.

### 2.2 Registration Requirements

**Step 1: Name Reservation**
- Submit 3 proposed names to Registrar General
- Ensure name reflects mission
- Cost: GHS 50-100
- Timeline: 1-3 days

**Step 2: Prepare Documents**
- Constitution/Articles of Incorporation
- List of founding members (minimum 2)
- List of directors/trustees (minimum 2)
- Registered office address
- Statement of guarantee (nominal amount)

**Step 3: Submit Registration**
- Registrar General's Department
- Forms 3 & 4 (Company details)
- Constitution document
- Filing fees
- Cost: GHS 500-1,000
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks

**Step 4: Post-Registration**
- Obtain Tax ID from GRA
- Apply for tax-exempt status (if applicable)
- Register with Department of Social Welfare (for NGO status)
- Open bank account
- Cost: GHS 500-1,000
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks

**Step 5: Additional Permits**
- District Assembly operating permit
- Ghana Education Service partnership (if working with schools)
- Cost: GHS 500-2,000
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks

## 3. PROGRAM DESIGN

### 3.1 Curriculum Overview

**Level 1: Basic Computer Literacy (4-6 weeks)**
- Introduction to computers
- Operating systems (Windows, basic Linux)
- File management
- Keyboard and mouse skills
- Basic troubleshooting
- Internet basics
- Email usage
- Digital safety and citizenship

**Level 2: Productivity Software (6-8 weeks)**
- Microsoft Word / Google Docs
- Microsoft Excel / Google Sheets
- Microsoft PowerPoint / Google Slides
- Document formatting
- Basic data analysis
- Presentation skills
- Printing and scanning

**Level 3: Digital Skills for Employment (8-10 weeks)**
- Advanced Office applications
- Online job searching
- Resume/CV creation
- Email professionalism
- Video conferencing (Zoom, Teams)
- Cloud storage (Google Drive, OneDrive)
- Basic graphic design (Canva)
- Social media for business

**Level 4: Specialized Tracks (10-12 weeks)**

*Track A: Entrepreneurship*
- Business plan writing
- Digital marketing
- E-commerce basics
- Mobile money and digital payments
- Record-keeping software
- Customer relationship management

*Track B: Technology Skills*
- Basic coding (Python, HTML/CSS)
- Web development basics
- Database fundamentals
- IT support skills
- Computer maintenance
- Network basics

*Track C: Creative Skills*
- Graphic design (Canva, Adobe basics)
- Video editing
- Content creation
- Photography basics
- Social media management
- Digital storytelling

*Track D: Agriculture Technology* (Synergy with your farm!)
- Farm management software
- Market price apps
- Weather and climate tools
- Agricultural information systems
- Mobile apps for farmers
- Digital record-keeping for farms

### 3.2 Training Delivery Models

**Model 1: Fixed Learning Centers**
- Dedicated computer lab in community
- 10-20 computers per center
- Regular class schedules
- Certified trainers
- Best for: Larger communities, long-term presence

**Model 2: Mobile Training Units**
- Laptop kits transported to communities
- Flexible locations (schools, community centers, churches)
- Reach remote areas
- Lower infrastructure cost
- Best for: Smaller communities, pilot programs

**Model 3: School Partnerships**
- Use existing school computer labs
- After-hours or weekend classes
- Leverage existing infrastructure
- Access to student population
- Best for: Youth programs, cost efficiency

**Model 4: Community Hub Model**
- Partner with existing community centers
- Shared space and resources
- Community ownership
- Lower operational costs
- Best for: Sustainable community integration

**Recommended:** Start with **Model 1 + Model 3** (fixed centers + school partnerships) for Year 1, add Model 2 (mobile) in Year 2 for remote areas.

### 3.3 Class Structure

**Standard Class:**
- Duration: 2-3 hours per session
- Frequency: 2-3 times per week
- Class size: 10-15 students (1 computer per 2 students maximum)
- Instructor: 1 certified trainer + 1 assistant (for classes >10)
- Duration: 4-12 weeks depending on level

**Flexible Scheduling:**
- Morning sessions (for students, unemployed youth)
- Afternoon sessions (for school students)
- Evening sessions (for working adults)
- Weekend sessions (for all groups)

**Certification:**
- Attendance requirement: 80%+
- Practical assessment
- Written test (basic levels)
- Project demonstration (advanced levels)
- Certificate of completion
- Digital badge (optional)

### 3.4 Pricing & Accessibility

**Tiered Pricing Model:**

| Category | Price (GHS) | Subsidy |

| **Productivity Software** | 150-250 | 50-70% subsidized |

**Scholarship Programs:**
- 30% of seats reserved for full scholarships
- Priority: Women, persons with disabilities, extreme poverty
- Application process with community verification
- Funded through donations and grants

**Payment Options:**
- Full payment (5% discount)
- Installment plans (2-3 payments)
- Mobile money (MTN MoMo, Vodafone Cash, AirtelTigo)
- Sponsor-a-student program (donors fund specific students)

## 9. RISK MANAGEMENT

### 9.1 Key Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|--------|------------|
| **Funding shortfall** | Medium | High | Diversified funding, phased expansion, reserve fund |
| **Low enrollment** | Medium | High | Community engagement, needs assessment, flexible pricing |
| **Equipment theft/damage** | Medium | Medium | Security measures, insurance, community ownership |
| **Power/internet outages** | High | Medium | Backup power, offline content, flexible scheduling |

### 9.2 Sustainability Risks

**Grant Dependency:**
- Risk: Operations collapse if grants end
- Mitigation: Develop earned income streams, build reserve fund

**Founder Dependency:**
- Risk: Foundation struggles without founder
- Mitigation: Build strong board, develop leadership pipeline, document processes

**Technology Obsolescence:**
- Risk: Equipment becomes outdated
- Mitigation: Regular refresh cycle, partnerships with tech companies, focus on transferable skills
