SWOT Analysis

Strengths

  • Proven model (3 locations, 12+ years)
  • 25.7% catering revenue — proven institutional model
  • Unique cuisine = zero direct competition
  • All-halal = growing demand driver
  • 17.1% 3rd-party delivery (DoorDash, Uber, Grubhub)

Opportunities

  • Woodbury 12% growth = rising dining demand
  • No African-Med fusion competitor
  • Catering already proven at 25.7% — bigger market
  • Dinner only 16.1% of existing sales — huge runway
  • Second location or Minnetonka in Yr 3–5

Weaknesses

  • First suburban expansion — new territory
  • Limited Woodbury brand awareness vs nationals
  • Royalty + marketing (8%) compresses margins
  • No drive-thru (vs Panera, Chipotle)
  • Owner-operator learning curve

Threats

  • Economic downturn — rising rates could cut discretionary spend
  • Halal protein inflation — chicken, beef prices volatile
  • Labor market pressure — wage inflation in Twin Cities metro
  • Potential Cava entry — national chain could enter Woodbury
  • Lease escalation — 20–40% increase at 5–10 year renewal
  • Supply chain — specialty African ingredient disruption

($8,400 + $5,075) ÷ 0.33 = $40,833 (≈ 105 covers/day)

Investment Options: $600K vs $400K

Two capital structures compared. Recommendation: $600K for proper capitalization.

✅ Recommended: $600,000

  • Equity: $200K (33%) | SBA Loan: $400K
  • Working Capital: $87K (6 months)
  • Peak drawdown of $13.8K → 6.3× coverage
  • Full build-out, new equipment, generous contingency
  • 6-month runway for any ramp delay or economic headwind
  • 63% debt-to-equity — conservative leverage

⚠ Lean Option: $400,000

  • Equity: $100K (25%) | SBA Loan: $300K
  • Working Capital: $14K (3 months only)
  • Peak drawdown of $13.8K → only 1× coverage
  • Used/second-gen equipment, leaner build-out
  • Risk: Underfunded by Month 5 without capital injection
  • Still viable with a second-gen space and experienced contractor
The $400K gap is all working capital. Cutting it from 6 months to 3 saves $73K but exposes the business to significant risk if the ramp takes 1 month longer than expected. The $600K plan is the safer path.
