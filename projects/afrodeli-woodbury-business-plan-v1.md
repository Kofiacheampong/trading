## 2. Company Overview

### 2.1 The Brand

Afro Deli is a fast-casual African-Mediterranean fusion restaurant concept rooted in the Twin Cities. Founded as a social enterprise, it weaves African, Mediterranean, and American cuisine in a fast, family-friendly environment. All dishes are made fresh and prepared halal.

- **3 operating locations** in the Twin Cities metro, **12+ years** of continuous operation
- **$1.37M combined annual net sales** across existing locations
- **25.7% of revenue from catering** — a proven institutional model
- **17.1% of revenue from third-party delivery**
- Regular presence at the **Minnesota State Fair** — deep local brand recognition
- **Social enterprise mission:** community hiring, cultural programming, and halal food access — a genuine differentiator from commercial operators

### 2.2 The Franchise Model

This plan covers the **first suburban franchise unit** of Afro Deli — Woodbury, MN. The franchise agreement includes a standard structure of 6% royalty + 2% marketing fund (to be verified against the FDD), franchisee training, approved supplier list, and territorial rights (Woodbury-specific, to be confirmed).

**Why franchise vs. independent:** the franchisor provides brand recognition, proven recipes and SOPs, an established catering playbook, buying power on specialty ingredients, and training — while the operator avoids the multi-year trial-and-error of concept development. The model's unit economics are already validated by three real locations (Section 13).

## 3. Industry Analysis

### 3.1 Fast-Casual Restaurant Sector

- The US restaurant industry continues to outpace grocery, with **fast-casual the fastest-growing segment** — driven by quality/price balance and the "elevated convenience" trade-up from QSR
- Unit economics favor **limited-menu concepts with catering and delivery** — both of which Afro Deli already operates (25.7% and 17.1% of sales)
- **Off-premise (delivery + catering + takeout) now exceeds 50% of industry sales** at the margin — Afro Deli's existing channel mix is structurally aligned with where the industry is going

### 3.2 Halal Food Market

- The global halal food market is projected to exceed **$2T by 2030**, growing faster than conventional food
- The US halal restaurant segment is **undersupplied relative to demand** — halal certification is a trust signal that commands loyalty, not a niche
- Minnesota is home to **one of the largest Somali/East African communities in the US** (estimated 50,000–80,000 in the Twin Cities metro) — an anchor customer base with deep cultural connection to the menu, plus a broader health-conscious and Muslim-family audience

### 3.3 Catering & Business Dining

- US business/corporate catering is a **multi-billion-dollar annual market**, and office return-to-work trends favor food-service vendors with established B2B relationships
- Afro Deli's existing locations derive **25.7% of revenue from catering** — an institutional playbook (medical campuses, corporate offices, schools, community events) that transfers directly to Woodbury's larger institutional base

### 3.4 Twin Cities Dining Context

- The metro is a **top-10 US food-city market** with strong independent-restaurant culture and growing ethnic cuisine demand
- Suburban dining density lags urban cores — suburbs like Woodbury are **under-served for differentiated ethnic concepts**, which is precisely the gap this plan exploits

## 4. Market Analysis — Woodbury

### 4.1 Why Woodbury? — Market Selection Rationale

Three Twin Cities suburbs were evaluated: **Woodbury**, **Minnetonka**, and **Edina**. Woodbury is the clear winner:

**1. Population & Growth (Critical Mass)** — ~75,000 residents, growing +12% since 2020 — three times faster than Minnetonka (+4%), four times faster than Edina (+3%). New households are actively forming dining habits; a new concept entering now captures the "shaping" of those habits.

**2. Affluence Without Saturation** — median household income $117,000 (≈ Edina's $120K, above Minnetonka's $105K), but restaurant density per capita is significantly lower: Edina has 186+ restaurants for 54,000 residents; Woodbury ~140 for 75,000. More people, fewer options.

**3. The Daytime Surge Advantage** — a 30–40% daytime population surge from Woodwinds Health Campus (major medical employer), the I-94 corporate corridor (3M, Ecolab, State Farm regional offices), and regional retail power centers. This drives lunch traffic AND catering demand — both validated by existing locations (55.7% lunch / 25.7% catering).

**4. Catering Infrastructure is Pre-Built** — Woodbury's institutional base (medical, corporate, schools) is larger and more corporate-heavy than the existing locations serve. The 25.7% catering model transfers directly.

**5. Zero Direct Competition** — no African-Mediterranean fusion, halal-first positioning, or cultural storytelling concept exists in the market (Section 5).

**6. Real Estate Economics** — lease rates $20–$25/sq ft NNN are 10–30% below Minnetonka and 20–40% below Edina; permitting takes 4–6 weeks vs. 6–12 weeks elsewhere. Lowest entry cost, fastest path to opening.

**7. Expansion Sequencing** — Woodbury (Yr 1) → Minnetonka (Yrs 3–5) → Edina express (Yr 5+): from largest market with lowest barriers to most expensive last, funded by Unit 1 cash flow.

## 5. Competitive Analysis

### 5.1 Direct Competitors (Woodbury)

| Competitor | Category | Price Point | Key Strength | Afro Deli's Advantage |
|-----------|----------|-------------|--------------|----------------------|
| Naf Naf Grill | Mediterranean FC | ~$11–13 | Established brand | African spicing, sambusas, cultural story |
| Chipotle | Mexican FC | ~$10–12 | Speed, price, loyalty | Unique flavor, not a commoditized burrito |
| Panera Bread | Bakery-Café | ~$11–14 | Breakfast/lunch, drive-thru, catering | Dinner-centric, bolder flavors, halal |
| Noodles & Co | Global FC | ~$10–12 | Broad family appeal | Authentic cuisine, halal commitment |
| Crisp & Green | Health FC | ~$11–13 | Health halo | Broader menu — flavor first |

**Positioning:** none of the five offer African-Mediterranean fusion, halal-first certification, cultural storytelling, or sambusas/Chicken Fantastic/Somali rice bowls. The gap is structural, not marginal. Afro Deli's $15 average ticket sits at a premium to QSR competitors — justified by a differentiated, higher-perceived-value menu and validated by existing locations' actual $15.89 ticket.

### 5.2 Catering Competition

Woodbury catering options are dominated by generic corporate caterers and chain catering menus (Panera, Chipotle). None offer **halal, culturally authentic African-Mediterranean catering at scale** — the specific ask from medical campuses, schools, and corporate offices with diverse workforces. Existing locations already win this business in St. Paul/Minneapolis; Woodbury's institutional base is larger.

### 5.3 Competitive Threats

- **Cava** (Mediterranean fast-casual) is expanding nationally and could enter Woodbury — its Mediterranean positioning overlaps, but it does not compete on African fusion, halal certification, or cultural identity
- **Independent imitation** within 2–3 years of success — mitigated by brand equity, catering relationships, and franchise system scale
- **Response:** first-mover advantage + relationship-based catering contracts are defensible moats
