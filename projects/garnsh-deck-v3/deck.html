MENU PUBLISHING FOR MULTI-LOCATION RESTAURANT GROUPS

Garnsh

One menu. Every location. Published on purpose.

A controlled publishing layer for restaurant groups on Toast: prepare the menu once, preview every surface, publish per location, and roll back if something is wrong.

Investor Deck v3.0 · November 2026 · Pre-revenue
01
GARNSH
THE PROBLEM

One menu. Four surfaces.
None guaranteed to agree.

A 10-location group maintains one menu in Toast and presents it on the website, a hosted page, QR codes and in-store boards — usually by hand.

  • Price drift Toast says $16.25; the website says $14.50.
  • Dead items Discontinued items and last season’s menu linger for weeks.
  • Wrong location One location’s prices or items appear at another.
  • No safety net A bad publish leaves a blank board during service.
02
GARNSH
CUSTOMER

10–50 location Toast groups
with a brand to protect.

  • ICP Regional fast-casual and casual-dining groups, all on Toast, with a marketing or operations owner for menu presentation.
  • Buyer VP of Marketing, Director of Operations, or the founder-operator.
  • Trigger A price change, rebrand, new location, menu cycle or agency handover.
  • Why now Menu data is a discovery asset, signage is cheap, and labor turnover keeps recreating the manual updater.

Below 10 locations the pain is real but the budget is small. Above 50 the sales cycle exceeds a two-person team.

03
GARNSH
SOLUTION

Publish once. Preview everything.
Roll back if wrong.

One menu document

Ingest from Toast or CSV/Sheets. Normalize items, modifiers, prices and location variants.

Per-location control

Map every surface to the right location, with overrides and a group dashboard.

Publishing safety

Preview, deliberate publish, version history, rollback and last-known-good serving.

Gen 1 is menu publishing. Not ordering, payments, live inventory or real-time sync.

04
GARNSH
PRODUCT

Four surfaces. One source of truth.

The workflow: ingest → normalize → map → preview → publish → serve.

  • Website embed The group’s own site, styled to the brand.
  • Hosted page A Garnsh-hosted menu per location.
  • QR menu A phone view opened by a printed code.
  • Menu boards In-store screens fed from the same published version.

Current stage: working demo. Production build Q1 2027. No customer screenshots are presented as live.

05
GARNSH
DIFFERENTIATION

The moat is publishing discipline,
not menu rendering.

  • Version history Every publish is recorded; one click rolls back.
  • Last-known-good A broken upstream source serves the last good menu, not a blank board.
  • Per-location mapping Prices, items and overrides follow the location, not the template.
  • Audit trail Brand teams and agencies can see who changed what, and when.

Agencies are the real incumbent. Toast native is view-only. Ordering suites treat menus as plumbing.
