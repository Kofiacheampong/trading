## 1. PURPOSE

This biosecurity protocol establishes measures to prevent the introduction and spread of diseases in our poultry farm. Effective biosecurity protects bird health, ensures productivity, and safeguards our investment.

**Goal:** Zero disease outbreaks through prevention.

### 3.2 Vehicle Access

**Restricted Vehicles:**
- No vehicles from other farms allowed

## 4. PERSONNEL HYGIENE

### 4.1 Daily Entry Procedure

**Step 1: Arrival at Farm Gate**
- Report to office/security
- Sign visitor/entry log
- Declare any contact with other poultry (last 48 hours)

**Step 2: Change Clothing (Yellow Zone)**
- Remove outdoor clothing and shoes
- Store in personal locker
- Put on farm-provided clothing and boots
- Cover hair with cap/hairnet

**Step 3: Hand Washing**
- Wash hands with antibacterial soap (20 seconds minimum)
- Dry with disposable towel
- Apply hand sanitizer

**Step 4: Foot Bath**
- Step through foot bath (both feet)
- Ensure boots are fully submerged
- Enter Red Zone only after foot bath

### 4.2 Protective Clothing

**Required PPE by Zone:**

| Item | Green Zone | Yellow Zone | Red Zone |
|------|------------|-------------|----------|
| Farm coveralls | No | Yes | Yes |
| Farm boots | No | Yes | Yes |
| Hair cover | No | Optional | Yes |
| Gloves | No | Optional | Yes |
| Face mask | No | Optional | Yes (if sick) |

**Clothing Management:**
- Farm clothing washed on-site (not taken home)
- Boots disinfected daily
- Damaged PPE replaced immediately
- Personal items not brought into Red Zone

### 4.3 Hand Washing Stations

**Locations:**
- Farm entrance
- Each poultry house entrance
- Office
- Feed storage

**Requirements:**
- Running water (or container with tap)
- Antibacterial soap
- Disposable towels
- Hand sanitizer

**When to Wash Hands:**
- Upon entering farm
- Before entering poultry house
- After handling birds
- After using toilet
- Before eating or drinking
- When leaving farm

## 5. POULTRY HOUSE MANAGEMENT

### 5.1 All-In-All-Out System

**Principle:** Each house contains birds of the same age, all entering and leaving together.

**Benefits:**
- Breaks disease cycle
- Allows thorough cleaning between batches
- Easier disease monitoring
- Better production planning

**Procedure:**
1. Day 1: All chicks placed in house
2. Week 1-8: Birds grow together
3. Week 8: All birds sold/removed
4. Days 1-14: House cleaned, disinfected, rested
5. Repeat with new batch

**Never:**
- Mix different age groups in same house
- Add new birds to existing flock
- Remove some birds while keeping others

### 5.2 Cleaning & Disinfection (Between Batches)

**Step 1: Remove Litter (Day 1-2)**
- Remove all litter material
- Transport to compost area (away from houses)
- Sweep all dust and debris

**Step 2: Dry Clean (Day 3)**
- Remove all feed from feeders
- Remove all equipment
- Sweep ceilings, walls, floors
- Remove cobwebs

**Step 3: Soak (Day 4)**
- Soak all surfaces with water
- Let sit for 2-4 hours
- Loosens dirt and organic matter

**Step 4: Wash (Day 5)**
- Wash with detergent solution
- Use pressure washer (if available)
- Scrub all surfaces
- Rinse thoroughly

**Step 5: Disinfect (Day 6)**
- Apply approved disinfectant
- Cover all surfaces (floor, walls, ceiling, equipment)
- Follow manufacturer's contact time
- Allow to dry completely

**Step 6: Rest (Day 7-14)**
- Keep house empty for 7-14 days
- Prevent entry of wild birds, rodents
- Allow disinfectant to work
- Break disease cycle

**Step 7: Prepare for New Batch (Day 15)**
- Install clean equipment
- Add fresh litter (4-6 inches deep)
- Pre-heat house 24-48 hours before chicks arrive
- Final inspection by Farm Manager

### 5.3 During Production

**Daily Cleaning:**
- Remove dead birds immediately
- Clean spilled feed
- Adjust feeders and drinkers
- Monitor litter condition

**Weekly Cleaning:**
- Clean around feeders and drinkers
- Remove wet litter spots
- Check and clean ventilation

**Monthly:**
- Clean exterior of house
- Clear drainage around house
- Inspect for rodent activity

## 6. DISEASE PREVENTION

### 6.1 Vaccination Program

**Standard Broiler Vaccination Schedule:**

| Day | Vaccine | Method | Notes |
|-----|---------|--------|-------|
| Day 1 | Marek's | Spray (at hatchery) | Usually done by hatchery |
| Day 7 | Newcastle (Lasota) | Eye drop / Drinking water | First dose |
| Day 14 | Gumboro (IBD) | Drinking water | Intermediate strain |
| Day 21 | Newcastle (Lasota) | Drinking water | Booster |
| Day 28 | Gumboro (IBD) | Drinking water | Booster |

**Vaccination Best Practices:**
- Use vaccines from reputable sources
- Store vaccines properly (cold chain)
- Use within expiry date
- Follow manufacturer's instructions
- Use clean, chlorine-free water for water vaccination
- Remove vaccines from water after 2 hours
- Record all vaccinations

### 6.2 Health Monitoring

**Daily Observation:**

| Sign | Normal | Warning | Action |
|------|--------|---------|--------|
| Activity | Active, alert | Lethargic, huddled | Investigate |
| Eating | Good appetite | Reduced feed intake | Check feed/water |
| Drinking | Normal | Increased/decreased | Check water |
| Droppings | Brown/grey | Bloody, watery, green | Isolate, call vet |
| Breathing | Quiet | Gasping, coughing, sneezing | Isolate, call vet |
| Mortality | 0-5 per day | >10 per day | Investigate immediately |

**Weekly Monitoring:**
- Weigh sample birds (5-10% of flock)
- Compare to breed standards
- Adjust feeding if needed
- Record all data

**Mortality Recording:**
- Count and record dead birds daily
- Note age/weight of dead birds
- Note any symptoms observed
- Store dead birds properly (see Section 7)

### 6.3 Early Warning Signs

**Immediate Action Required If:**
- Mortality exceeds 1% per day
- Feed consumption drops by 10% or more
- Water consumption changes significantly
- Birds show respiratory signs (coughing, sneezing)
- Birds show nervous signs (twisted necks, paralysis)
- Bloody droppings observed
- Sudden drop in activity

**Action Steps:**
1. Isolate affected birds/house
2. Contact veterinarian immediately
3. Restrict farm access
4. Increase biosecurity measures
5. Begin diagnostic testing
6. Implement treatment if prescribed

## 7. MORTALITY MANAGEMENT

### 7.1 Dead Bird Removal

**Daily Procedure:**
- Walk through house each morning
- Remove all dead birds
- Place in sealed, leak-proof container
- Transport to mortality storage area

**Mortality Storage:**
- Dedicated freezer or sealed pit
- Located away from poultry houses
- Prevent access by scavengers
- Empty regularly (weekly minimum)

### 7.2 Disposal Methods

**Option 1: Burial Pit (Recommended)**
- Dig pit at least 2 meters deep
- Located at least 100 meters from poultry houses
- Add lime layer at bottom
- Place dead birds in pit
- Cover with lime and soil
- Mark location

**Option 2: Incineration**
- Use approved incinerator
- Located away from poultry houses
- Burn completely to ash
- Bury ash

**Option 3: Composting**
- Dedicated composting area
- Layer dead birds with litter and carbon material
- Maintain high temperature (kills pathogens)
- Use finished compost away from farm

**Never:**
- Leave dead birds in poultry house
- Dump dead birds in open areas
- Feed dead birds to other animals
- Sell or give away dead birds

## 8. PEST CONTROL

### 8.1 Rodent Control

**Prevention:**
- Seal all holes and gaps (>6mm)
- Keep feed in sealed containers
- Remove spilled feed immediately
- Keep vegetation trimmed around houses
- Remove clutter and debris

**Monitoring:**
- Install bait stations around perimeter
- Check weekly
- Record rodent activity
- Replace bait as needed

**Control:**
- Use approved rodenticides
- Place in tamper-proof bait stations
- Locate away from bird access
- Record all bait usage
- Dispose of dead rodents properly

### 8.2 Wild Bird Control

**Prevention:**
- Install bird netting on openings
- Close doors when not in use
- Remove standing water (attracts birds)
- Keep feed covered
- Use bird deterrents (reflective tape, etc.)

**Monitoring:**
- Check for wild bird entry daily
- Look for wild bird droppings
- Report any wild birds inside houses

### 8.3 Insect Control

**Darkling Beetles:**
- Remove litter between batches
- Apply insecticide to house surfaces
- Allow proper downtime between batches

**Flies:**
- Manage wet litter
- Remove manure regularly
- Use fly traps/baits
- Keep area clean

## 9. WATER BIOSECURITY

### 9.1 Water Quality

**Testing:**
- Test water quality monthly
- Check for: pH, bacteria, minerals
- Acceptable pH: 6.5-7.5
- Total bacteria: <1,000 CFU/ml

**Treatment:**
- Chlorinate water if needed (3-5 ppm)
- Use water sanitizers between batches
- Flush lines between batches

### 9.2 Water System Maintenance

**Daily:**
- Check water pressure
- Inspect for leaks
- Ensure all drinkers functioning

**Weekly:**
- Flush water lines
- Clean drinker cups/bells
- Check water meters

**Between Batches:**
- Drain all water lines
- Clean with detergent
- Disinfect with approved product
- Flush thoroughly before new batch

## 10. FEED BIOSECURITY

### 10.1 Feed Storage

**Requirements:**
- Dedicated feed storage building
- Rodent-proof construction
- Pallets to keep bags off floor
- First-in-first-out (FIFO) rotation
- Keep bags sealed until use

**Inspection:**
- Check for mold or off-odors
- Look for signs of pests
- Reject damaged bags
- Record feed deliveries

### 10.2 Feed Handling

**Best Practices:**
- Use clean equipment for feed handling
- Don't return feed from house to storage
- Clean feeders between batches
- Prevent feed spillage

**Monitoring:**
- Track feed consumption daily
- Investigate sudden changes
- Record feed batch numbers

## 11. EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### 11.1 Disease Outbreak Response

**Immediate Actions:**
1. Isolate affected house(s)
2. Stop all movement on farm
3. Contact veterinarian immediately
4. Notify Farm Manager/Owner
5. Increase biosecurity to maximum
6. Begin diagnostic testing

**Movement Restrictions:**
- No birds in or out
- No feed delivery (unless emergency)
- No visitors
- Staff stay on farm if possible

**Communication:**
- Inform all staff of situation
- Notify authorities if required (notifiable diseases)
- Update neighboring farms
