# 📋 How to Read an Earnings Report — Cheat Sheet

*One page. The numbers that decide the reaction, and the traps that get people burned.*

---

## 1. The Scoreboard (check in this order)

| # | Number | Why it matters | Good vs Bad |
|---|--------|----------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Revenue vs consensus** | The single biggest driver of the reaction. A miss here kills stocks even if profit beats. | Beat = fine. Miss = problem, no matter what else |
| 2 | **EPS (adjusted) vs consensus** | What the street anchors on. But read which EPS — see trap #2. | Beat good, but adjusted EPS lies |
| 3 | **Guidance (next quarter + full year)** | Moves the stock MORE than the quarter itself. Market trades the future. | Raised = rally fuel. Cut = selloff. "In line" = meh |
| 4 | **Margins (gross + operating)** | Growth with falling margins = selling dollar bills for 90 cents. Margin expansion = operating leverage. | Expanding = quality growth. Shrinking = red flag |
| 5 | **Cash flow / balance sheet** | Profit is opinion, cash is fact. Debt-funded growth = risk. | Positive FCF + low debt = durable. Negative FCF + debt = fragile |

**Reaction cheat code:**
- Beat revenue + raised guidance = 🚀 (the good kind)
- Beat EPS + missed revenue = ⚠️ (market often sells anyway)
- Missed both = 💀 (don't catch the knife)
- Beat both but stock drops = it was **already priced in** (see trap #1)

---

## 2. The 6 Traps

1. **"Priced in" is the #1 killer.** A great quarter means nothing if the street expected great. Compare to *consensus*, not to "was it good." Stock reaction = surprise, not quality.
2. **Adjusted vs GAAP EPS.** "Adjusted" strips out stock comp, one-offs, impairments. Check the reconciliation. A company can report "adjusted profit" while GAAP is a loss (Cerebras: -$0.05 adjusted vs $450M net loss). Both are true; know which one you're trading.
3. **Core vs total revenue.** Companies increasingly report "core" (real business) and "total" (includes pass-through/markup revenue). Media headlines grab the bigger number. Always find core.
4. **One-time items in either direction.** Big tax benefit, asset sale, or stock-comp charge can flip the EPS. Read the *why*, not just the sign.
5. **Backlog is not revenue.** A huge backlog (RPO) is real demand *contracted*, but it converts slowly and can be cancelled/renegotiated. Great for confidence, worthless for this quarter.
6. **After-hours ≠ final.** AH moves are thin-liquidity prints. The real verdict is the next morning's open and how it holds for the first 30–60 min. Don't panic-sell at 10 PM on a $2 spread.

---

## 3. The 3 Questions Before You Trade It

1. **Was it a surprise?** (vs consensus, not vs last year)
2. **Did guidance move?** (up/down/flat — this is the real signal)
3. **Was it priced in?** (had the stock run 30% into the print? Then even a beat can sell off)

If all three line up (surprise + raised guide + not priced in) → that's the trade.
Any one fails → at most a watch, not a position.

---

## 4. Post-Earnings Playbook (your rules)

- **Never chase the gap.** Post-earnings pops pull back 10–20% before the next leg (HNST rule). The gap is the market's opinion; your entry is your own.
- **No earnings straddle** in the momentum scanner — defined risk only, no weekend/event holds.
- **Earnings dates get verified** against company IR before any play (VERIFY BEFORE ASSERT — the INTC lesson).
- **IPO earnings swing hardest** (CBRS: 3 months public, -14% AH on a guide-up). Treat as spec, not position.
- If you didn't do the homework before the print, you don't get to trade the reaction — you get to *learn* from it.
