2. Strategic changes

2.1 Gen 1-only base case. Removed ordering ($99/location) and app ($149/location plus $5,000 build) revenue from Scenario A. They were 17%/28% and 3.5% attach in v3.0 and made the 2028–29 numbers look like a different company. Scenario B now shows them separately, with explicit development cost ($75,000 in 2028; $70,000 in 2029), certification dependency and launch dates (July 2028 and July 2029).

2.2 Design-partner program cut from 12 months to 90 days. The v3.0 offer — four groups free for a year, six at 20% for a year, setup waived for all ten — cost about $91,286 in foregone revenue and labor against a $20,000 balance sheet. The v3.1 program costs about $30,595: four groups free for 90 days, six at 20% for six months, setup waived only for the four free groups. The trade-off (90 days may be short for a publishable case study) is stated in the plan.

2.3 Setup fee changed from a flat $1,500 to $120 per location, $1,500 minimum. The flat fee lost $180 at 14 locations and $3,300 at 40. The three requested alternatives are modelled in the plan; cost-recovery is recommended and adopted.

2.4 One customer funnel. v3.0 used 10 design partners, 6 discounted, 4 free, 15 gross groups, 40 conversations and 60 conversations in different sections. v3.1 uses one chain: 60 qualified conversations → 24 paid pilots → 15 gross groups (4 free, 6 discounted, 5 full price) → 14.0 net groups → 192 live and paying locations. The arithmetic error ("40 conversations produce 15 customers") is removed; 15 groups at a 25% rate requires 60 conversations.

2.5 Market evidence re-labelled. The 189,000 Toast locations, +21% and $2.5B ARR are verified against Toast's Q3 2026 results. The 10–20% multi-unit share remains a management estimate, now explicitly labelled MODELED, with the research that would improve it named.

2.6 Competition rebuilt. Marqii is reclassified from "partner or acquirer" to an adjacent competitor with published per-location pricing. Pricing is shown with its basis (per location, per brand, per restaurant, quote-based) and a verification date. Claims about Toast's native tools are narrowed to what Toast documents: a view-only website menu channel and limited customization, not "a bad menu experience". BentoBox's Clover-only status and its pricing remain secondary-source claims and are labelled as such.

2.7 Toast section rewritten. v3.0 mixed up standard access, partner access and the legal right to use customer credentials. v3.1 separates the three routes, cites the four Toast developer documents with access dates, and states that public-facing use of customer-supplied credentials is unverified. The day-120 gate now forces a route decision rather than "approval in hand or in process".

2.8 Founder section completed with placeholders, not invention. Names, backgrounds, restaurant experience, time commitment and equity split are marked [FOUNDER INPUT REQUIRED]. Role responsibilities and decision rights are drafted so the founders only need to confirm or change them.

2.9 Plan shortened. 18 pages to 15, with detailed schedules moved to appendices. Repetition of the product boundary, the Toast caveat and the reserve policy was cut to one statement each plus one cross-reference.

4. Claims removed or qualified

Removed

  • "CAC exceeds LTV" at the proposed price points.
  • "Forty conversations produce ten design partners" and the implied 15 groups from 40 conversations.
  • The description of Marqii as a potential partner or acquirer rather than a competitor.
  • The implication that Toast Websites cannot produce a suitable menu experience.
  • Any suggestion that Toast approval, certification or marketplace access exists.
  • The claim that six showcase groups and four design partners are separate programs.
  • The 2028–29 revenue, profit and ARR figures that included ordering and app revenue.

Qualified

  • "LTV:CAC 38.4x" is now labelled MODELED and described as an assumption set, not a benchmark.
  • "Gen 1 SAM $13.8M–$27.7M" is labelled MODELED, with the 10–20% input identified as a management estimate.
  • All competitor prices carry a basis and a date; secondary sources are marked.
  • "Last-known-good serving" is described as designed, not built.
  • The design-partner case study is a contractual obligation and a target, not a promised outcome.
  • The 2027 funnel is labelled TARGET throughout.

5. Deck changes

Rebuilt from the plan rather than reworded:

  • 15 slides, one message each.
  • Financial slide replaced with Gen 1-only figures and an explicit note that paid 2027 onboarding exhausts the capital.
  • Validation slide shows zeros (signed, live, paying) next to the 2027 target, so the deck cannot be mistaken for traction.
  • Competition slide now shows price basis and avoids per-location vs per-restaurant comparisons presented as equivalent.
  • Unit-economics slide labels the 38.4x ratio as modeled and carries the 1.3-month payback.
  • Team slide uses placeholders; no founder biographies were invented.
  • No product screenshots are used, because none exist. The deck describes the workflow and states which capabilities are demo-only.

7. What did not change

  • The positioning: menu publishing and control for multi-location restaurant groups.
  • The Gen 1 product boundary: no ordering, payments, inventory, loyalty or real-time sync.
  • The customer profile: 10–50 location Toast groups.
  • The $59 core price and $40 board price.
  • The $20,000 capitalization and the $5,000 reserve floor.
  • The conclusion that the first test is whether groups will pay for Gen 1 at all.
