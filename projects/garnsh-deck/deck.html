THE PROBLEM

One menu. Five versions.
None of them agree.

The menu a guest sees is never the menu the kitchen runs. It is a copy of a copy, edited by whoever had the login.

  • Fragmentation The POS holds the real menu. The website holds a PDF from March. The QR code points at a third copy. The boards run off a USB stick.
  • Multi-location Groups multiply it — airport, stadium and flagship pricing on the same item, each with its own stale copy.
  • Cost The guest reads $16 and gets charged $18. That is a comp, a bad review, and in some jurisdictions a consumer-protection exposure.
  • Turnover Someone retypes the menu into Canva every time a price changes. Then that person quits.

WHY NOW

The menu became the product listing.

  • Machine-readable AI answer engines and search summaries reward current, structured menus. A PDF is invisible to them.
  • Hardware collapsed A 43" commercial display is a few hundred dollars. The bottleneck is software and content, not screens.
  • Labor churn "The person who knew how" is now a recurring annual failure mode in every restaurant.
  • Compounding base Toast adds ~8,500 net locations a quarter. Our addressable market grows without us spending a dollar.

WHAT IT IS

Four surfaces. One source of truth.

Hosted page

A public menu page per location, server-rendered, phone-first, in the restaurant's own brand.

Website embed

Drops the same menu into the site they already have. No rebuild, no migration.

QR menu

A phone view behind a printed code, one per location — tables, patios, counters.

Menu board

A designed display for screens they already own. 16:9 and 3-panel landscape.

The operator keeps running the menu in Toast. We handle what guests see, where, and for which location.

GARNSH
THE DIFFERENCE

We ship menus like software.

Nobody in restaurant tech treats a menu like a release. That is the entire product.

  • Preview → publish → rollback Draft v15 is never visible while v14 is live. Every published version is retained and restorable.
  • Last-known-good If the upstream feed breaks, surfaces keep serving the most recent version that published correctly. No blank boards at the counter.
  • Location mapping Each surface is bound to one location. The airport price never appears on the flagship board.
  • Freshness, honestly "Source last read 6 minutes ago" — an operational readout, not a fake real-time claim.

SCOPE

What we refuse to build — and why.

No ordering. No payments. No cart. No delivery. No 86 status.

  • Off Toast's revenue path We never touch orders or money, so we are never competing with the platform we depend on.
  • Out of PCI scope No card data, no chargebacks, no tax and tip logic. A different company, with different risk, is what we are deliberately not.
  • No conflict for the operator We do not threaten their ordering vendor, so saying yes costs them nothing politically.
  • Focus Every engineering hour goes to the hard part: correct, versioned, per-location rendering.

Branded ordering experience that submits into the operator's existing Toast Online Ordering. Not our own checkout.
