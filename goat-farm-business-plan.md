## 1. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

### West Africa Goat Market
- **Nigeria** has the largest goat population in Africa (~88 million head), followed by Ethiopia (~49 million)
- **Ghana's goat population:** ~8 million head (USDA 2023 estimate)
- **Goats are the #1 small ruminant in West Africa** — more common than sheep in most countries
- The goat meat market in Ghana alone is worth roughly **$2.5M/year** in recognized trade, with much more flowing through informal markets

### Demand Drivers
1. **Cultural staple** — Goat meat (chevron) is central to ceremonies, festivals, weddings, and religious events across Ghana and West Africa. Demand spikes during Eid, Christmas, and traditional celebrations
2. **Urbanization** — Rising urban populations in Accra, Kumasi, and regional capitals are driving demand for animal protein
3. **Import gap** — Ghana imported **$5.44M of sheep and goat meat in 2024**, becoming the 69th largest importer globally. Domestic production isn't keeping up
4. **Price increases** — Live goat prices in Ghana have been rising steadily. Goat meat retails around **GHS 40-90/kg** depending on location and season

### Supply Side Reality
- Most goats in Ghana are raised under **traditional extensive systems** — low input, low output
- Herd sizes are small (5-20 goats per household)
- Mortality rates are high due to disease, poor nutrition, and lack of veterinary care
- **Formal commercial goat farming is rare** — this is your competitive advantage

### Key Statistic
> Ghana's mutton and goat meat market volume was **47,000 metric tonnes in 2023** and has been growing steadily. Global goat meat market is projected to grow from **$248B (2026) to $317B (2035)** at 2.8% CAGR. The goat farming market specifically is growing at **5.3% CAGR** globally.

## 2. WHY BOER GOATS

| Trait | Local Breeds (West African Dwarf) | Boer Goat |
|-------|-----------------------------------|-----------|
| Adult weight (male) | 25-35 kg | 90-120 kg |
| Adult weight (female) | 20-30 kg | 70-90 kg |
| Age to market weight | 12-18 months | 8-10 months |
| Kidding rate | 1-2 kids | 1-3 kids (often twins) |
| Meat yield | Low | High, lean, premium |

**Bottom line:** A Boer goat reaches market weight twice as fast and produces 3x the meat of a local breed. This is the difference between subsistence and profit.

## 3. THE BUSINESS — PHASED ROLLOUT

### Phase 1: Setup (Months 1-3)
**Goal:** Prepare land and infrastructure before goats arrive

- **Fencing:** ~400m of goat-proof chain-link or welded wire, 4ft minimum. Goats are escape artists. Budget ~GHS 2,000-3,000
- **Shelter:** 3-sided roofed structure, raised wooden floor (prevents pneumonia), good drainage. Bamboo frame + aluminium sheets. Budget ~GHS 1,500-2,000
- **Water:** Clean water source near shelter. Gravity-fed or bucket system is fine for 5 goats
- **Browse:** Do NOT clear all vegetation. Goats eat bushes, weeds, shrubs. Plant moringa, leucaena, and gliricidia along fence lines — free protein feed
- **Vet contact:** Find a livestock vet in Nsawam or Suhum area before buying. Set up a relationship

### Phase 2: Stocking (Month 3)
**Purchase:** 1 Buck + 4 Does (5 goats total)

- Good quality Boer goat prices in Ghana: **GHS 1,500-3,000/head**
- Buy from a reputable breeder. Visit the farm. Check for: broad chest, strong legs, good muscling, clear eyes, clean coat, calm temperament
- Recommended breeders: Farming in Africa (they supply Boer/Kalahari crosses), or contact via +233 (0) 545
- Transport: Hire a pickup or small truck with a covered bed

### Phase 3: First Year Operations (Months 3-15)

**Health Schedule:**
| Event | Frequency |
|-------|-----------|
| Deworming | Every 6-8 weeks |
| PPR vaccination | Annually (critical) |
| Hoof trimming | Every 3-4 months |
| Vitamin/mineral boost | Quarterly |

**Breeding:**
- Does cycle every 21 days
- Gestation: ~5 months (150 days)
- First kidding: ~Month 8 (if you breed Month 3)
- Expected: 4-8 kids from 4 does (1-2 per doe)

**Feeding:**
- 70% browsing/forage (free on your 3 acres)
- 30% supplement: concentrate mix (corn, wheat bran, palm kernel cake), mineral salt lick, clean water
- Monthly feed cost for 5 goats: ~GHS 300-500

**Record Keeping:**
- Start a simple logbook or spreadsheet:
  - Goat ID / tag number
  - Birth date
  - Weight (monthly)
  - Vet visits
  - Breeding dates
  - Kids born
  - Sales

### Phase 4: Growth & Revenue (Months 12-24)

**Herd Growth Projection:**

| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Starting does | 4 | 4 | 6-8 |
| Kids born | 6-8 | 10-14 | 18-24 |
| Herd size (end) | 9-11 | 16-20 | 25-35 |
| Goats sold | 2-4 (surplus males) | 6-10 | 12-18 |

**Revenue Streams:**
1. **Meat sales** — Live goats at 8-10 months. Sell at Nsawam market, to butchers, restaurants. Live weight ~40-60 kg per goat
2. **Breeding stock** — Sell young does/bucks to other farmers at premium prices
3. **Manure** — Bag and sell. Goat manure is excellent organic fertilizer
4. **Skins** — Sell hides to local tanneries (secondary, but adds up)

**Pricing (Ghana Market 2026):**
- Live goat (local breed, adult): GHS 800-1,500
- Live Boer goat (adult): GHS 1,500-3,000
- Boer breeding doe: GHS 2,000-3,500
- Boer buck: GHS 3,000-5,000
